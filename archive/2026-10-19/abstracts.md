### The endocannabinoid system and the ageing brain.
_Zimmer A · 2026 Oct 16_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-026-01085-7) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42888282/)

Ageing is accompanied by a progressive decline in homeostatic resilience that increases vulnerability to chronic disease, disability and cognitive impairment. Although unhealthy brain ageing can often be prevented by managing the underlying risk factors, the molecular, cellular and physiological mechanisms that connect risk factors to late-life brain dysfunction remain incompletely understood. This review examines the role of the endocannabinoid system in brain ageing. The endocannabinoid system is a short-range lipid signalling network that helps to maintain neuronal, glial, metabolic and inflammatory homeostasis. Endocannabinoid signalling follows a dynamic lifespan trajectory, with relatively high activity during early adulthood and functional decline during ageing. Genetic or pharmacological disruption of endocannabinoid system activity impairs several processes linked to hallmarks of brain ageing, including neuroinflammatory control, adult neurogenesis, synaptic plasticity, cognitive performance, metabolic regulation and epigenetic stability. Conversely, low-dose Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) treatment can ameliorate several ageing-related brain phenotypes in old animals. However, clinical evidence in older humans remains limited, and the therapeutic potential of cannabinoid-based interventions requires further evaluation.

### An aging-sensitive secreted phospholipase confers neuroprotection and cognitive resilience.
_Vicidomini C, Goode TD, Yu R, Mehta I, McAvoy KM, Beveridge CH, Randolph CE, Iyer SN et al. · 2026 Oct 15_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02465-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42881225/)

Cognitive reserve theory posits that compensatory mechanisms during aging stave off cognitive decline and lower risk for Alzheimer's disease. Here, in a screen for hippocampal dentate granule cell (DGC) synapse loss-induced factors in mice, we identified a secreted phospholipase, Pla2g2f, whose expression increases in DGCs during aging and is associated with cognitive resilience in humans. Pla2g2f deletion in DGCs exacerbates aging-associated pathophysiological changes, including synapse loss, inflammatory microglia, reactive astrogliosis, impaired neurogenesis, lipid dysregulation, lipid droplet accumulation and hippocampal-dependent memory loss. Conversely, boosting Pla2g2f in DGCs during aging is sufficient to prevent these pathophysiological alterations in hippocampal-cortical circuits, protect memory and prophylactically modify trajectory of cognitive decline. Mutagenesis of the catalytic site of PLA2G2F prevented lipid droplet mobilization and reduction of reactive gliosis and inflammatory microglia. Neuronal PLA2G2F mediates intercellular signaling to decrease lipid droplet and cholesterol ester load in human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived microglia. Our findings implicate PLA2G2F as a compensatory neuroprotective broadcast signal that maintains lipid homeostasis to counteract aging-associated cognitive decline.

### A half-center oscillator regulates sleep pressure.
_Hasenhuetl PS, Sarnataro R, Vrontou E, Rorsman HO, Talbot CB, Brain R, Miesenböck G · 2026 Oct 15_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02431-9) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42881223/)

Changes in oscillatory neural dynamics reflect-but may not necessarily determine-transitions between sleep stages and variations in sleep pressure. Here we show that sleep-control neurons innervating the dorsal fan-shaped body of Drosophila (dFBNs) produce slow-wave activity (SWA) that is causally linked to sleep. The dFBN ensemble contains bistable cells whose membrane voltages oscillate between hyperpolarized DOWN and depolarized UP states. The oscillations rely on interhemispheric competition of two half-centers connected by inhibitory glutamatergic synapses. dFBN-restricted interference with glutamate release disrupts SWA and baseline as well as rebound sleep, whereas the optogenetic replay of SWA induces sleep. dFBNs generate SWA throughout the sleep-wake cycle (despite mutual antagonism with arousing dopaminergic neurons) and adjust its power to sleep need via sleep history-dependent increases in excitability and homeostatic synaptic depression. The oscillatory format permits a continuous encoding of sleep pressure but requires mechanisms that convert the amplitude-modulated signal into binary sleep-wake states.

Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)-adjacent brain areas are especially vulnerable to diffuse degeneration in multiple sclerosis (MS). ### Symmetries and continuous attractors in disordered neural circuits.
_Clark DG, Abbott LF, Sompolinsky H · 2026 Oct 14_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02452-4) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42875514/)

A core challenge in neuroscience is reconciling idealized models with experimental data. Classical continuous-attractor models describe neural representations of continuous variables such as head direction. Symmetry in the weights generates a manifold of stable states, with tuning curves identical up to shifts. Rodent head-direction cells, by contrast, are markedly heterogeneous. Such heterogeneity, we show, is nevertheless compatible with these models. Here we build, directly from head-direction cell data, recurrent networks that behave as quasicontinuous attractors. A generative process then reproduces the heterogeneous tuning of these cells while making connectivity and dynamics tractable at large N. Although the weights appear disordered, they carry a circular symmetry whose eigenvalue degeneracies could signify continuous-attractor organization in connectome data. Dynamical mean-field theory recovers the ingredients of classical ring attractors, namely, Mexican hat interactions and a spontaneously broken symmetry producing bump states. Continuous-attractor mechanisms may thus operate in complex mammalian circuits.

### Investigating molecular and hemodynamic effects of LSD in the human brain.
_McCulloch DE, Larsen K, Johansen A, Reveles Jensen KH, Nykjær CH, Holze F, Falck N, Neufeld V et al. · 2026 Oct 12_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02462-2) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42864445/)

Lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) is a potent psychedelic with increasing therapeutic interest, yet qualification of target engagement using in vivo neuroimaging has not yet been performed. Using simultaneous positron emission tomography-magnetic resonance imaging technology, we describe multiple brain actions of LSD in seven healthy volunteers blinded to LSD dose received. Here we show that LSD occupies 5-HT2AR with a high affinity and increases both global cerebral blood flow (CBF) and internal carotid artery flow. LSD also decreases global connectivity, which was negatively associated with changes in CBF. ### Neuronavigated dual-target transcranial magnetic stimulation for schizophrenia negative symptoms.
_Wei Y, Wang J, Tang X, Qian Z, Xu L, Cui H, Song Y, Zhu H et al. · 2026 Oct 15_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag359) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42879192/)

Negative symptoms in schizophrenia severely impair function and quality of life, yet respond poorly to current therapies. This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial aimed to investigate the efficacy of neuronavigated dual-target repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) over the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (rDLPFC) and cerebellum for improving negative symptoms in early-stage psychosis (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04853485). Participants with first-episode schizophrenia or clinical high-risk for psychosis were enrolled from Shanghai Mental Health Center and Suzhou Guangji Hospital. Participants were randomly assigned to either a real or sham group under MRI-guided neuronavigation, targeting rDLPFC with 1Hz at 100% motor threshold(MT), cerebellar with intermittent theta burst stimulation(iTBS) at 120% MT for 20 consecutive days. Clinical assessments were conducted at baseline(T0), 10-session(T1), 20-session(T2) and one month after the intervention(T3). Cognition and MRI were evaluated at T0 and T2. The primary outcome was change of negative subscale score of the Positive and Negative Symptoms Scale(PANSS), while secondary outcomes included alterations in other PANSS subscale scores and fronto-cerebellar circuit functional connectivity. In total, 75 participants were included in the intention-to-treat analysis (36 active, 39 sham). The pre-specified primary linear mixed-effects analysis revealed a non-significant group-by-time interaction for PANSS negative subscale scores (F(3,174.6)=1.45, p=0.230). At the 4-week endpoint (T2), the nominal unadjusted between-group p-value was 0.049; after Sidak correction for multiple comparisons, the adjusted mean difference was -2.48 points (95% CI -4.99 to 0.03, adjusted p=0.150, Cohen's d=-0.51). Resting-state fMRI demonstrated group-by-time interactions within cerebello-temporal, parahippocampal, caudate, and parietal networks (FWE-corrected p<0.05), and negative-symptom change correlated with cerebello-caudate connectivity (r=0.33, p=0.026). These findings suggest neuronavigated dual-target rTMS over rDLPFC and cerebellum may be associated with numerical improvements in negative symptoms in early-stage psychosis.

### Extracellular vesicle α-synuclein topology reveals disease-associated conformers in brain and blood.
_Dutta S, Atashgaran A, Sahoo A, Chatzimichail S, Kapanidis AN, Tofaris GK · 2026 Oct 14_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag356) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872588/)

Parkinson's disease (PD), dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), and multiple system atrophy (MSA) are unified by pathological α-synuclein (αSyn) aggregation, yet diverge in terms of the cellular distribution of pathology and clinical phenotype. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) have emerged as potential mediators or biomarkers of αSyn pathology, but whether their molecular constituents in the brain reflect disease-specific processes or conformations remains unknown. Here, we combined EV isolation, electrochemiluminescence, quantitative mass spectrometry and nanoscale single-vesicle imaging by direct stochastic optical reconstruction microscopy (dSTORM) to map the topology, epitope exposure and cellular origin of αSyn in EVs isolated from post-mortem PD, DLB, MSA, and healthy control (HC) brains and assessed the tractability of these molecular signatures in blood. Proteomics and immunoblotting of EV lysates isolated from human brains confirmed that they were highly purified and derived from neurons and glial cell subtypes. This N-terminal immunoreactive conformer was tractable in serum and enriched on the surface of L1CAM+ EVs from MSA compared to PD patients or controls. Collectively, our findings suggest that the epitope accessibility and topology of EV-associated αSyn proteoforms, rather than their abundance alone, discriminate synucleinopathy subtypes, offering a framework for a blood-based conformational stratification of these disorders.

### Biallelic variants in PIKFYVE cause a hypomyelinating leukodystrophy with endolysosomal dysfunction.
_Averdunk L, Groß M, Hamacher AB, Donkervoort S, Herebian D, Mohammad S, Knaus A, Seibt A et al. · 2026 Oct 14_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag355) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872583/)

The PIKfyve-FIG4-VAC14 lipid kinase complex generates phosphoinositide PI(3,5)P2. Pathogenic variants in VAC14 and FIG4 cause neurodevelopmental disorders and PIKFYVE has been linked to autosomal dominant fleck corneal dystrophy. We searched for the underlyging, genetic cause of a neurodevelopmental disorder in five affected individuals from three families using exome- or genome sequencing. Our findings establish PIKFYVE deficiency as a novel cause of hypomyelinating leukodystrophy and endolysosomal dysfunction.

### Target engagement and efficacy in disease-modifying trials of synuclein-related disorders.
_Artusi CA, Gallo S, Galli S, Rinaldi D, Tinazzi M, Rascol O, Espay AJ, Fabbri M · 2026 Oct 12_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag353) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42861702/)

Disease-modifying trials in Parkinson's disease (PD), multiple system atrophy (MSA), and dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) increasingly incorporate fluid and imaging biomarkers to demonstrate target engagement, de-risk subsequent phases of drug development and provide mechanistic evidence of biological activity. Whether biomarker modulation predicts clinical benefit remains unclear. We conducted a PRISMA-compliant review of phase II and III disease-modifying trials (2020-2025) that included at least one biomarker intended to assess target engagement. Twenty-six trials met inclusion criteria (PD=19, MSA=4, DLB=3; n=2,195 participants). Target engagement was reported in 14 of 19 PD trials (12 phase II/2 phase III), 1 of 4 MSA trials, and 2 of 3 DLB trials (all phase II). Concordance between target engagement and clinical improvement was uncommon, as only 8/26 studies (7 phase II/1 phase III) demonstrated both target engagement and statistically significant clinical benefit, and just two of these (both phase II, one in PD and one in MSA) were rigorously controlled with low or moderate risk of bias. Nine trials showed target engagement without clinical benefit, whereas two reported clinical improvement without measurable target engagement. Baseline biomarker-guided patient selection was uncommon and rarely aligned with the compound's mechanism of action. Patient-reported outcomes were widely used but rarely improved. Across recent disease-modifying trials in PD, DLB, and MSA, target engagement was frequently demonstrated, yet clinical benefit rarely followed. These findings question the predictive value of currently used target-engagement biomarkers and highlight the need to biologically align trial populations with the mechanisms targeted by experimental molecular interventions.

### Salivary Dim-Light Melatonin Onset is Associated with Functional Decline, Respiratory Symptom Emergence, and Survival in Early Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis.
_Bombaci A, Iadarola A, Giraudo A, Fattori E, Sinagra S, Magnino A, Falbo M, De Marco G et al. · 2026 Oct 16_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78398) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42885660/)

**OBJECTIVE.** Sleep-wake and circadian disturbances are increasingly recognized in people living with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (plwALS), but endogenous circadian phase timing and its prognostic significance in early disease remain unclear. We assessed whether salivary dim-light melatonin onset (DLMO), an objective marker of central circadian phase, is altered in early plwALS and whether it provides prognostic information.

**METHODS.** In this prospective longitudinal observational study, plwALS within 18 months of symptom onset underwent home-based salivary melatonin sampling under dim-light conditions at 6 predefined time points around habitual sleep onset. Melatonin profiles were modeled using cubic smoothing splines, and DLMO was defined when the fitted curve first reached 3 pg/ml. Clinical, respiratory, and sleep assessments were collected at baseline (T0) and after 6 months (T6); a subgroup repeated saliva sampling at T6. Age- and sex-matched controls underwent melatonin profiling. Associations with disease progression, incident respiratory symptoms, and survival/tracheostomy were examined using regressions and survival analyses.

**RESULTS.** A total of 50 plwALS were enrolled. Compared with controls, plwALS showed an earlier DLMO (20:24 ± 1:18 vs 20:58 ± 0:50; p = 0.028) despite similar habitual sleep onset and chronotype. Within ALS cohort, a later baseline DLMO correlated with worse functional/motor status, faster progression of disease, incident dyspnea/orthopnea by T6 (adjusted OR 3.11; p = 0.019), and poorer survival/tracheostomy-free outcome. In the resampled subgroup (n = 28), DLMO and melatonin-derived metrics did not change over ~6 months.

**INTERPRETATION.** Circadian phase alterations are detectable in early ALS. Baseline DLMO showed exploratory associations with functional, respiratory, and survival outcomes. Larger independent cohorts are required before DLMO can be considered a validated prognostic biomarker. ANN NEUROL 2026.

### Mild Amyloid Burden in the Brains of Cognitively Unimpaired Individuals is Associated with Long-Term Cognitive Impairment.
_Xiong C, Luo J, Hartz S, Schindler SE, Kukull W, Hassenstab J, Wolk DA, Gleason CE et al. · 2026 Oct 14_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.78392) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42873601/)

**OBJECTIVE.** We aimed to identify the minimal amount of amyloid burden measured by positron emission tomography (PET) in amyloid-negative cognitively unimpaired individuals that predicts the development of cognitive impairment.

**METHODS.** A total of 1,896 cognitively unimpaired participants underwent longitudinal clinical/cognitive assessments for up to 20.3 years after amyloid PET imaging. Cox proportional hazards models were used to assess how the risk of cognitive impairment was associated with subthresholds of amyloid PET Centiloid. Linear mixed effects models were used to associate the longitudinal cognitive decline and amyloid accumulation with baseline amyloid burden.

**RESULTS.** Amyloid-positive participants (Centiloid >20; n = 421) were 154% (hazard ratio 2.54, p < 0.0001) more likely to develop cognitive impairment than amyloid-negative participants. Approximately 48.7% of the amyloid-negative participants had mild amyloid burden (defined by Centiloid from 0.93 to 20) and were 43% more likely to develop cognitive impairment (hazard ratio 1.43, p = 0.008) than the lowest amyloid group (Centiloid <0.93) approximately 10 years post baseline. Longitudinal changes in amyloid PET Centiloid and cognition followed a similar pattern: amyloid-positive participants were the fastest, followed by the mild amyloid burden group and finally the lowest amyloid group, and the cognitive trajectories for the latter 2 started to separate only approximately 10 years post baseline (p = 0.003).

**INTERPRETATION.** Almost half of amyloid-negative cognitively unimpaired individuals were at an elevated long-term risk of cognitive impairment, when compared with those with the lowest amyloid levels. **DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS.** This retrospective cohort study used data from the AM-PREGNANT cohort, derived from the Merative MarketScan Commercial Database, from 2018 to 2021. Participants were followed up through 20 weeks' gestation or until spontaneous abortion or censoring. Among 7 991 200 pregnancies identified in the AM-PREGNANT cohort, 7119 individuals contributing 7579 singleton pregnancies met the predefined eligibility criteria, including age 15 to 45 years, continuous insurance enrollment, last menstrual period (LMP) on or after November 1, 2018, pregnancy end or 20-week censoring by December 31, 2021, and pregestational migraine, defined as a migraine diagnosis or triptan or ergot prescription within 6 months before LMP. Data were analyzed in early 2026.

**EXPOSURES.** Anti-CGRP mAbs (erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab) or propranolol, defined as 1 or more filled prescription or injection procedure claim during follow-up and modeled as time-varying.

**MAIN OUTCOME AND MEASURES.** Spontaneous abortion occurring at 20 weeks' or less gestation. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models adjusted for comorbidities and concomitant medications estimated the risk of spontaneous abortion.

**RESULTS.** Among 7579 pregnancies in 7119 individuals (mean [SD] age, 31.6 [5.5] years), 1862 spontaneous abortions occurred. There were 100 spontaneous abortions (28.6%) during anti-CGRP mAb-exposed person-time, compared with 37 events among propranolol-exposed person-time without anti-CGRP mAbs (12.8%). Compared with unexposed individuals, anti-CGRP mAbs exposure was associated with an increased risk of spontaneous abortion (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 1.42; 95% CI, 1.12-1.80), which remained similar in bias-adjusted analyses (HR, 1.33; 95% simulation interval [SI], 1.23-1.45). Propranolol exposure was not associated with increased risk (aHR, 1.02; 95% CI, 0.71-1.47; bias-adjusted HR, 1.00; 95% SI, 0.96-1.03).

**CONCLUSION AND RELEVANCE.** In this study, early pregnancy exposure to anti-CGRP mAb was associated with a higher risk of spontaneous abortion, while no association was observed with propranolol. These findings support further evaluation of anti-CGRP mAb safety and emphasize counseling for individuals of childbearing potential.

### Global Incidence of All-Cause Dementia: A Meta-Analysis.
_Vella AS, Visontay R, Lipnicki DM, Nichols E, Steinmetz JD, Vos T, Nguyen ATH, Llibre-Rodriguez JJ et al. · 2026 Oct 12_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.3540) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42862582/) · [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13659630/)

**IMPORTANCE.** Global dementia incidence estimates are limited by underrepresentation of low- and middle-income countries and heterogeneous analytic methods. Due to these limitations, true understanding of the global dementia landscape remains incomplete.

**OBJECTIVE.** To identify dementia incidence rates by age and sex in various regions worldwide (high-income countries and low- and middle-income countries) where data on clinically based dementia diagnoses were available.

**DATA SOURCES.** Retrospective community-based cohorts from the Cohort Studies of Memory in an International Consortium (COSMIC); baseline recruitment commenced between January 1990 and December 2011 and follow-up data were collected through the end of 2021. Data were analyzed from June 2024 to June 2025.

**STUDY SELECTION.** Eligible studies included longitudinal COSMIC cohort studies with a clinically based diagnosis of dementia. Individual participant-level data from 22 population-based longitudinal cohorts (N = 38 418) spanning 18 countries or territories across 6 continents were included.

**DATA EXTRACTION AND SYNTHESIS.** Data extraction and assessment of study quality and validity were conducted according to Meta-Analyses of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (MOOSE) guidelines. Individual participant-level data were meta-analyzed in a 2-step process using random intercepts for country and study at the global and regional levels and by country income level.

**MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES.** For each study with a clinically based diagnosis, incidence rates for all-cause dementia were calculated and a 2-step meta-analysis was conducted. Using a person-years at risk framework with inverse-probability censoring weights and Poisson models, age- and sex-specific incidence were estimated and then standardized to the 2023 global age-sex population structure.

**RESULTS.** Across 287 632 person-years at risk, 3001 participants developed dementia. Age- and sex-standardized incidence was 9.7 (95% CI, 7.89-11.97) per 1000 person-years at risk. Age-standardized incidence was higher in women than men (10.6; 95% CI, 8.61-13.11 vs 8.6; 95% CI, 6.94-10.72). Standardized incidence was higher in low- and middle-income countries (13.6; 95% CI, 9.67-19.24) than in high-income countries (8.3; 95% CI, 6.57-10.54). Applying age- and sex-specific rates to the projected 2050 population yielded a projected 24.7 million new cases, assuming constant incidence over time.

**CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE.** The harmonized estimates presented in this study provide a comparable benchmark for global dementia incidence and inform planning for prevention, diagnosis, and memory-care services, particularly in underrepresented low- and middle-income country settings.
