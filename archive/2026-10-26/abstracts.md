### Mitochondrial dysfunction in neurology: biomarkers and clinical implications.
_Rossini E, Garibaldi M, Hanna MG, Pitceathly RDS, Pizzamiglio C · 2026 Oct 23_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-026-01274-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919989/)

Mitochondrial dysfunction is increasingly recognized as a shared downstream pathogenic mechanism across a wide spectrum of neurological disorders, ranging from primary mitochondrial diseases to conditions including amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, Alzheimer disease, Parkinson disease, multiple sclerosis, hereditary ataxias and idiopathic inflammatory myopathies. This Review examines the role of mitochondrial biomarkers in improving diagnostic accuracy, patient stratification and monitoring of disease progression and therapeutic response. Primary mitochondrial diseases are presented as proof-of-concept models of impaired oxidative phosphorylation, providing a framework for biomarker identification and validation with translational relevance to other neurological diseases. Mitochondrial biomarkers can be broadly categorized into four domains: fluid biomarkers, reflecting metabolic alterations and stress signalling; genetic biomarkers, capturing mitochondrial DNA integrity and dynamics; multi-omics approaches, integrating transcriptomic, proteomic and metabolomic signatures; and imaging biomarkers, enabling in vivo assessment of structural and functional consequences of mitochondrial dysfunction. Across disorders, these biomarkers reveal shared disturbances in bioenergetics, redox homeostasis, mitochondrial genome maintenance and inflammatory signalling. However, clinical implementation remains limited by methodological heterogeneity, biological variability and reduced disease specificity. Integrated, multimodal biomarker strategies, combined with longitudinal validation, will be essential for supporting precision medicine and guiding the development of therapies to target mitochondrial pathways in neurology.

### Multi-organ imaging genetics: towards an integrated view of brain, body and brain diseases.
_Fan Z, Zhao B · 2026 Oct 19_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41582-026-01276-x) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42897328/)

Population-based cohorts that combine genetic data with whole-body multimodal imaging are transforming how we map the genetic architecture of the brain in its broader bodily context. Historically, imaging genetics has focused largely on single organs, especially the brain, with separate efforts targeting the heart, skeleton and other organs for systemic diseases, and limited integration across organ systems. Yet, strong brain-body interactions and the high multimorbidity of brain and systemic diseases highlight the need for a brain-centred multi-organ perspective. Here we introduce the emerging field of brain-focused multi-organ imaging genetics, outlining key data resources and interdisciplinary analytical frameworks for large-scale multi-organ genetic discovery and reviewing the genetic associations between brain diseases and imaging phenotypes across organ systems. We discuss how these advances support a whole-body integrated view of brain health and disease and how they are beginning to reshape clinical translation for neurological conditions. We outline a roadmap for agentic artificial intelligence co-scientists that work alongside human researchers throughout the scientific discovery process, arguing that such systems will be essential for achieving an integrated understanding of brain health and disease, both as tools for individual researchers and as a collaborative infrastructure for the whole field.

### Interneurons and network activity in the cerebral cortex: a developmental dialogue.
_Moissidis M, Marín O · 2026 Oct 23_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-026-01088-4) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919967/)

The mammalian cerebral cortex undergoes profound functional changes during early postnatal development. During this period, neurons generate spontaneous electrical activity long before sensory experience, coordinating into distinct patterns of synchronous activity. Traditionally, neuronal activity has been viewed as instructing interneuron development by modulating transcriptional programmes, morphology and connectivity. However, accumulating evidence in rodents reveals that this relationship is bidirectional: cortical interneurons are not merely passive recipients of activity-dependent signals but also active architects of developing network dynamics. They regulate spontaneous activity transitions from gap-junction-mediated synchronous events to large, synaptically driven bursts, and later to sparse firing characteristic of mature circuits. Critically, different interneuron subtypes mature at distinct timelines and orchestrate transitions in network activity. Activity-dependent mechanisms, in turn, regulate interneuron wiring, programmed cell death, diversification and terminal differentiation. This reciprocal dialogue between interneurons and network activity is essential for establishing functional cortical circuits.

### Sustained antidepressant-like effects of psychedelics through 5-HT2AR-β-arrestin signaling.
_He L, Tang L, Cao D, Duan W, Yu J, Zhang P, Li H, Wang Y et al. · 2026 Oct 23_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02497-5) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919912/)

Psychedelics produce long-lasting antidepressant effects, but their hallucinogenic properties limit clinical use. Using structure-guided design of 5-HT2AR-β-arrestin-biased agonists, we identified β-arrestin signaling as a critical driver of prolonged antidepressant-like effects in mice. Here we show that the slow-dissociation kinetics of lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) enable progressive accumulation of 5-HT2AR engagement during repeated low-dose administration, approaching the receptor occupancy achieved by an acute high dose while avoiding its high peak concentration. This kinetic separation sustains β-arrestin signaling while limiting robust Gαq-mediated Ca2+ responses. Consistent with this framework, repeated low-dose LSD and repeated low-dose psilocybin pretreatment followed by a high-dose psychedelic challenge produced sustained antidepressant-like behavioral effects in mice. In parallel, these regimens produced no detectable psychedelic-associated behavioral disruption in mice and cynomolgus macaques. Together, these findings uncover the signaling basis of sustained psychedelic efficacy and provide a rational framework for developing nonhallucinogenic psychedelic therapies for psychiatric disorders.

### Identification of candidate markers for the isolation of neuron-specific extracellular vesicles.
_Ter-Ovanesyan D, Whiteman S, Gilboa T, Kowal EJK, Trieu W, Iyer S, Budnik B, Babila CM et al. · 2026 Oct 23_

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are present in biofluids and may be useful biomarkers to non-invasively monitor brain cells, but isolating cell type-specific EVs remains challenging. Here we introduce an approach to immuno-isolate EVs from neurons and glia based on protein surface markers. We first selected transmembrane proteins suitable as cell type-specific EV markers based on gene expression and EV proteomic data. ### Network geometry shapes the transformation of task representations across the human cortex.
_Chakravarthula LNC, Ito T, Tzalavras A, Cole MW · 2026 Oct 22_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02486-8) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42918864/)

Flexible cognition requires the brain to generalize across tasks while preserving task-specific information. Previous work showed that task representations are compressed in the association cortex and expanded in sensory and motor systems, but the mechanisms underlying these transformations are unclear. Here we test whether the geometry of intrinsic brain connectivity constrains how representations change between cortical regions. We analyzed functional MRI acquired during 16 diverse cognitive tasks together with activity flow modeling, finding that connectivity dimensionality predicts representational changes across the cortex. Low-dimensional connectivity compressed task representations and increased similarities across tasks, whereas high-dimensional connectivity expanded representations and supported conjunctive coding of task features. Activity flow modeling reproduced the observed compression-to-expansion pattern along the sensory-association-motor hierarchy and generated representational geometries that more closely matched targets than sources, consistent with transformation rather than transfer of information. These findings identify network geometry as a systems-level principle that shapes cortical representations and supports flexible cognition.

Somatosensation is mediated by dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, which convert physical and chemical stimuli into electrical signals. ### SIK3 controls synaptic localization of NMDA receptors by phosphorylating GluN1.
_Lu R, Morishita W, Han X, Temkin P, Malenka RC, Südhof TC · 2026 Oct 21_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02469-9) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42907494/)

Synaptic NMDA-type glutamate receptors (NMDARs) are central components of all neural computations. NMDAR levels critically vary between synapses but how the synaptic localization of NMDARs is controlled remains unclear. Here we show that SIK3, a serine-threonine protein kinase, localizes to synapses where it phosphorylates the obligatory NMDAR subunit GluN1 at a single site. This single phosphorylation event controls NMDAR recruitment to synapses. Genetic deletion of SIK3 severely and selectively decreases synaptic GluN1 levels by ~60% without altering AMPA-type glutamate receptor levels or inducing major transcriptional changes. Pharmacological inhibition of SIK3 impairs synaptic NMDAR responses within 45 minutes. Mutating the SIK3-phosphorylation site inhibits GluN1 synaptic recruitment. 'Sleepy', a gain-of-function mutation in SIK3 that increases sleep, selectively enhances synaptic NMDAR levels by increasing GluN1 phosphorylation. Thus, SIK3 phosphorylation of GluN1 provides a key signal for recruiting NMDARs to synapses.

Maternal caregiving requires sensory vigilance to ensure offspring survival, particularly during sleep. Although brain cholesterol dysregulation is implicated in Parkinson's disease (PD), targeting cholesterol synthesis has shown limited efficacy. ### An amygdala-mediated simulation-to-decision cascade aligns interbrain dynamics to solve social dilemmas.
_Ni J, Meng F, Zhang C, Tan H, Zeng X, Yang J, Li H, Qi Y et al. · 2026 Oct 19_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02473-z) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42897303/)

Mutual cooperation (MC) requires independent decision-makers to coordinate under uncertainty about each other's intentions. Using simultaneous intracranial electroencephalography from epilepsy-patient dyads playing a real-time iterated prisoner's dilemma, we identified neurocognitive mechanisms that enable interacting brains to align during mutual cooperation. Amygdala interbrain representational alignment (IBRA), similarity in fine-grained neural spectral patterns across partners, emerged selectively during MC and predicted trial-by-trial outcomes. These findings reveal the neural interdependency underpinning MC, redefining cooperation not as isolated decision-making but as a dynamic, interbrain, cognitive alignment.

### Spatiotemporal remodeling in habenula marks the onset of depression-related phenotypes.
_Molina P, Osypenko D, Garnica J, Amossé Q, Bouteldja F, Camelot L, Mondoloni S, Congiu M et al. · 2026 Oct 19_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02488-6) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42897302/)

Chronic stress has lasting adverse consequences that can lead, over time, to depression, yet the optimal timeframe for effective therapies remains unclear. The lateral habenula (LHb) has been implicated in stress-related behavioral repercussions, but the precise timing of its contribution is not understood. Here, using spatial transcriptomics, we generated a spatially resolved molecular atlas of the LHb of male mice, revealing a topographically organized and temporally defined remodeling of gene expression after chronic stress. ### Clinically unresponsive patients with acute brain injury activate a brain-computer interface.
_Shen Q, Eliseyev A, Kansara V, Neves Briard J, Song YL, Carmona J, Gonzales IJ, Velazquez A et al. · 2026 Oct 22_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag210) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42911846/)

Brain activation to verbal commands can be detected by EEG in clinically unresponsive patients. These patients with cognitive motor dissociation (CMD) are unable to communicate with the environment despite preserved consciousness. Advanced brain-computer interfaces (BCI) are promising to support motor function in patients with motor neuron disease or spinal cord injury. Our primary objective was to determine if clinically unresponsive patients can control a self-paced, one-way BCI early after acute brain injury. Secondarily, we assessed the association of BCI control with short- and long-term outcomes. We conducted a prospective single-centre cohort study in 41 clinically unresponsive, critically ill and acutely brain injured patients, as well as in a convenience sample of 8 healthy volunteers and 22 conscious critically ill patients. The self-paced BCI analysed EEG in real time and modulated frequency and pitch of a tone that patients controlled by attempting a motor task: patients were provided with instructions 'to make the beep sound faster, keep opening and closing your right hand' alternating with 'to make the beep sound slower, stop opening and closing your right hand'. CMD was detected by applying machine learning to EEG recorded while patients listened to motor commands. Clinical outcomes were recovery of consciousness by hospital discharge, assessed with the Coma Recovery Scale-Revised, and recovery of functional independence at 12 months, defined as a Glasgow Outcome Scale-Extended score of 4 or higher (denoting the ability to function independently for 8 h or more). Additionally, we invited conscious patients as well as family members of conscious and clinically unresponsive patients to complete a survey on perceived utility and challenges associated with the BCI. In this study, 13 clinically unresponsive patients (32%) achieved BCI control and 8 of 41 (20%) were CMD-positive. All healthy volunteers and nine conscious patients (41%) were able to control the BCI. Median signal delay for BCI control was 49.8 [interquartile range (IQR) 39.9-75.6] seconds for clinically unresponsive patients, 17.1 (IQR 7.8-19.9) for conscious patients and 15.5 (IQR 12.9-21.2) for healthy volunteers. In the group of 41 clinically unresponsive patients, 4 of 13 patients with (31%) and 1 of 28 without BCI control (4%) recovered clinical consciousness at hospital discharge [odds ratio (OR) 21.2; 95% confidence interval (CI) 2.3-930.7]. At 12 months, 6 of 13 patients with (46%) and 2 of 28 without BCI control (7%) recovered functional independence (OR 9.9; 95% CI 1.8-81.6). None of the patients that were unable to control the BCI and were CMD-negative recovered. Patients with BCI control had shorter times to recovery of functional independence by 12 months than those who did not (hazards ratio 6.0; 95% CI 1.2-30.6). All respondents of the BCI survey (n = 37) expressed strong support for the potential future use of BCI devices. These results suggest that BCI control is a strong predictor of outcomes following acute brain injury and provide initial evidence that a bedside, EEG-based BCI system might permit bidirectional communication in critically ill unresponsive patients.

### Progenitor transcriptional alterations link fetal and adult pathology in Huntington's disease.
_Battistella M, Damaggio G, Conforti P, Scolz A, Maestri S, Bocchi VD, Iafolla D, Galimberti M et al. · 2026 Oct 22_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag364) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42910642/)

Huntington's disease (HD) is an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by a CAG repeat expansion in the HTT gene. Although the mutant huntingtin protein is expressed from conception, clinical symptoms typically emerge in mid-adulthood. Imaging and molecular studies have raised the possibility that HD may involve neurodevelopmental abnormalities, but direct evidence at the single-cell level in the human fetal brain has been lacking. Here we investigated whether early, cell type-specific transcriptional vulnerabilities arise during neurodevelopment, persist into adult pathology, and might be rescued. To address this, we performed single-cell transcriptomic profiling of the lateral ganglionic eminence (LGE) and cortex from a rare, anatomically intact human HD fetal brain, alongside matched controls. These data were complemented by an allelic series of HD mouse embryos and cross-compared with already published human post-mortem brains and brain organoid models' datasets. Cross-comparison with publicly available post-mortem HD datasets revealed that components of this transcriptional signature persist into adulthood. Furthermore, cellular interactions were found to mitigate these alterations, highlighting a potentially rescuable fetal-to-adult transcriptional signature with important implications for HD pathogenesis and therapeutic strategies. Together, these findings reinforce the view of HD as a disorder with developmental origins, in which early transcriptional changes may shape neuronal function while increasing susceptibility to later neurodegeneration.

### Prematurity insults remodel cerebellar development and behaviour.
_Sanidas G, Simonti G, Ghaemmaghami J, Woyshner K, Vidva R, Bittel D, Wolff N, Triantafyllou M et al. · 2026 Oct 21_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag363) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42904924/)

Preterm survivors often develop motor and socio-cognitive impairments that implicate altered cerebellar development, yet the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. A key challenge is that prematurity involves overlapping perinatal insults that converge on the developing brain, making their individual effects difficult to disentangle. Here, we model two major prematurity-associated insults, maternal immune activation (MIA) and neonatal hypoxia (Hx), in mice. By controlling timing and sequence, we define how these insults shape cerebellar assembly during rapid maturation. Comparative analysis with human tissue confirmed that these insults recapitulate key features of the human preterm cerebellum, establishing the translational validity of this model for dissecting insult-specific outcomes. Behavioral and kinematic profiling revealed divergent motor and social phenotypes, which we traced to insult-specific cerebellar remodelling. Hypoxia compromised both granule cell maturation in the internal granule layer and the principal excitatory afferents to Purkinje cells, a circuit state that manifested as impaired motor execution and stereotyped, sensory-disengaged social investigation. Maternal immune activation, by contrast, expanded the granule cell progenitor layer and reduced Purkinje cell dendritic complexity, a phenotype that preserved social preference but reorganized the kinematic structure of social investigation. When hypoxia followed maternal immune activation, it acted on this primed substrate to produce a distinct state in which the features established by prior inflammation were compounded by granule cell proliferative arrest, progressive mitochondrial dysfunction, and aberrant, hyper-interactive social investigation. Together, these findings reframe prematurity-associated insults as cerebellar reprogramming events shaped by both the identity and sequence of insults, linking distinct mechanistic substrates to divergent neurodevelopmental outcomes.

### Serum β-synuclein predicts dementia conversion and mortality beyond amyloid-β and phospho-tau.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag361) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42904923/)

Whether serum β-synuclein provides prognostic information independent of established amyloid-β (A; CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio) and tau (T1; CSF pTau181) biomarkers has not been determined. Serum β-synuclein provided prognostic information largely independent of CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 across all endpoints and of pTau181 for survival outcomes, while sharing substantial prognostic variance with pTau181 for cognitive trajectory, supporting its candidacy as a blood-based synaptic complement to core Alzheimer's disease biomarkers.

### The clinical relevance of multiple sclerosis genetics: from susceptibility to disease severity and progression.
_Jensen LT, Gold R, McVean G, Attfield KE, Fugger L · 2026 Oct 23_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(26)00356-X) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42915008/)

Large-scale genome-wide association studies have identified hundreds of multiple sclerosis risk loci, most of which implicate immune pathways and establish multiple sclerosis as a highly polygenic, immune-mediated disease. These findings help explain why immune-modifying therapies suppress relapses and new lesion formation. However, susceptibility genetics does not adequately explain disability accumulation, variation in disease course, or treatment response. Evidence from rare-variant studies, genetic studies of disease severity and disability progression, epigenetic analyses, gene-environment studies, and Epstein-Barr virus research is beginning to extend this genetic framework from susceptibility to disease expression. Still, the direct clinical relevance of this research remains constrained by modest individual-level predictive value, underpowered studies of genetic determinants of disease severity and disability progression, complex regulatory mechanisms linking variants to function, and restricted generalisability across populations. At present, multiple sclerosis genetics is most useful for interpreting mechanisms, prioritising therapeutic targets, supporting causal inference, informing biomarker discovery, and enriching research cohorts. Future clinical value will likely depend on integrating genetic architecture with longitudinal clinical, imaging, biomarker, and multi-omic data to identify the contexts in which prevention or treatment is most likely to succeed.

### Risk of Inflammatory Bowel Disease After Anti-CD20 Treatment in Multiple Sclerosis.
_Kallin S, Svenningsson A, Olén O, Hedin C, Lycke J, Alping P · 2026 Oct 22_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.3439) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42911175/) · [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13691918/)

**IMPORTANCE.** Anti-CD20 biologics are widely used as disease-modifying therapy (DMT) for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS), but recent reports have raised concerns about an increased risk of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) during treatment. This study aimed to assess treatment-associated IBD risk in individuals with RRMS.

**OBJECTIVE.** To compare the risk of new-onset IBD across different therapies for RRMS and relative to the general population.

**DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS.** This was a population-based cohort study conducted in Sweden. Through the Swedish MS register, initiations of rituximab, natalizumab, dimethyl fumarate, and fingolimod were identified between January 1, 2011, and December 31, 2023, in individuals with RRMS aged 18 to 55 years with at least 10 years of lookback time (5 years for dispensed drugs) and no evidence of previous IBD. These initiations were matched with general population comparator episodes selected from the Swedish population.

**EXPOSURES.** Rituximab, comparator DMTs (natalizumab, dimethyl fumarate, fingolimod), or general population comparator.

**MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES.** New-onset IBD, defined as 2 or more records on separate dates with the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems, Tenth Revision, codes K50 (Crohn disease), K51 (ulcerative colitis), and/or K52.3 (indeterminate colitis).

**RESULTS.** Among the 13 748 therapy episodes (6481 rituximab, 2976 natalizumab, 2767 dimethyl fumarate, 1524 fingolimod) in 9251 individuals (range in mean ages of the exposure groups, 35-38 years) and 61 065 matched general population episodes, 164 IBD events occurred during follow-up (40 with rituximab, 4 with comparator DMTs, and 120 in the general population). The crude incidence rate of IBD was 110 (95% CI, 76-147) per 100 000 person-years for rituximab, 13 (95% CI, 3-30) for the comparator DMTs, and 41 (95% CI, 34-48) for the general population. After adjustment for demographics and MS characteristics, the hazard ratio for the comparator DMTs vs rituximab was 0.10 (95% CI, 0.04-0.22). In the rituximab group, 62% of IBD events were classified as Crohn disease compared with 29% in the general population. The number needed to harm for rituximab vs the comparator DMTs was 131 (95% CI, 93-228) at 5 years, after full adjustment.

**CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE.** Results of this cohort study show that rituximab was associated with an increased risk of IBD compared with other modern MS therapies, after adjustment for differences in characteristics at therapy start. Clinicians and individuals with MS should consider IBD risk in treatment decisions and remain vigilant for gastrointestinal symptoms during anti-CD20 therapy.

### Vascular Risk Factors and Late-Onset Epilepsy.
_Choi H, Gutierrez J, Wang T, Liu M, Leu CS, Misiewicz S, Han J, Bello NA et al. · 2026 Oct 19_
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.3708) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42895730/) · [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13681222/)

**IMPORTANCE.** Late-onset epilepsy (LOE; onset at age ≥65 years) is increasingly recognized in older adults and may be associated with vascular risk.

**OBJECTIVE.** To identify vascular risk factors (VRFs) associated with incident LOE in older adults.

**DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS.** This study is an individual participant data meta-analysis of 6 US population-based prospective cohort studies (the Epilepsy-Cog Study). In 5 cohorts linked to Medicare for epilepsy case ascertainment, follow-up for LOE began when a participant had accrued 2 years of continuous fee-for-service enrollment and was 65 years of age or older and extended to the end of continuous enrollment. Claims linkage covered 1991-2018 in the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities Study (ARIC), 1992-2019 in the Cardiovascular Health Study (CHS), 2000-2019 in the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA), 2000-2020 in the Reasons for Geographic and Racial Differences in Stroke Study (REGARDS), and 1999-2019 in the Washington Heights/Inwood Columbia Aging Project (WHICAP). In the Northern Manhattan Study (NOMAS), which was not Medicare linked and used cohort-based epilepsy case ascertainment, follow-up for LOE began at cohort entry in 1993-2008 and extended to 2024. Data analysis was completed in January 2026.

**EXPOSURES.** Sociodemographic characteristics and VRFs assessed by self-report, examinations, laboratory measures, and medication use.

**MAIN OUTCOME AND MEASURES.** The primary outcome was incident LOE. Associations between risk factors and incident LOE were evaluated using multivariable Cox proportional hazards models to estimate hazard ratios.

**RESULTS.** From 43 753 participants in Epilepsy-Cog, 551 participants with prevalent epilepsy and 945 with no follow-up after Epilepsy-Cog baseline were excluded. Among 42 257 participants (mean [SD] age, 71.4 [6.9] years; 23 383 [56.0%] female) followed up for a mean (SD) of 9.3 (6.2) years, 1500 participants (3.5%) developed LOE (3.8 per 1000 person-years). In fully adjusted models, LOE risk was higher with current smoking (HR = 1.39; 95% CI, 1.18-1.64), hypertension (HR = 1.17; 95% CI, 1.04-1.31), diabetes (HR = 1.42; 95% CI, 1.25-1.62), chronic kidney disease (HR = 1.29; 95% CI, 1.13-1.48), history of stroke (HR = 1.99; 95% CI, 1.63-2.43), and apolipoprotein E ε4 allele (1 ε4 allele: HR = 1.22; 95% CI, 1.07-1.39; 2 ε4 alleles: HR = 1.84; 95% CI, 1.36-2.50). Associations of VRFs with LOE were similar across age, sex, and race and ethnicity subgroups.

**CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE.** In this pooled analysis of 6 US cohorts, multiple VRFs and a history of stroke were associated with incident LOE. These findings suggest that LOE may reflect the underlying burden of brain vascular disease.
